--- SECTION 2 · WHAT IS A FRACTAL (en-02) ---

Imagine you draw a triangle. Then you split each side into three equal parts, and on
the middle segment you build a smaller triangle. [PAUSE] You repeat the process over
and over. At first it looks like a curious sketch; eventually, a shape that never
stops branching. [PAUSE] That is the famous Koch snowflake. It is not only beautiful:
it shows how something very simple can generate enormous complexity. [PAUSE] And the
strange part is that the closer you look, the more detail appears. It does not
simplify: it deepens.

--- SECTION 3 · NATURE (en-03) ---

Nature does not calculate on a chalkboard, but it does optimize. [PAUSE] Tree branches
distribute nutrients; leaf veins carry water; lungs increase surface area for oxygen.
[PAUSE] In every case there is branching: a structure that splits into smaller parts,
similar but not identical. [PAUSE] It is not magic. It is geometry adapted to life.
[PAUSE] So when you see a fractal on screen, you are not looking at something alien
to the real world. You are looking at an abstraction of something you already know,
even if you never named it that way.

--- SECTION 4 · SCALES (en-04) ---

Think about the coastline of a country. From an airplane it looks like a smooth line.
In a helicopter, bays and cliffs begin to appear. [PAUSE] If you walk along the shore,
every rock, every curve, every crack tells another story. [PAUSE] Depending on the
rule you use to measure, the length of that coast can grow without a practical limit.
[PAUSE] That reminds us of something important: scale changes what we see. Not only
in geography, but in data, in social networks, in the human brain.

--- SECTION 5 · CHAOS AND ORDER (en-05) ---

For a long time, order and chaos were treated as opposites. [PAUSE] Then chaos theory
arrived, with a disturbing idea: simple systems can be unpredictable in the long run.
[PAUSE] The classic example is the butterfly effect. A tiny change at the start can
radically alter the final outcome. [PAUSE] But notice: unpredictable does not mean
random. There is structure, there are attractors, there are patterns that appear when
you visualize millions of iterations. [PAUSE] That is where fractals and chaos meet:
order that looks like chaos, and chaos that hides order.

--- SECTION 6 · MANDELBROT (en-06) ---

We cannot talk about fractals without mentioning the Mandelbrot set. [PAUSE] The formula
is almost minimal: z squared plus c. That is it. [PAUSE] But if you repeat that
operation thousands of times for each point on the plane, a figure appears that looks
like a cosmic insect, with antennae, bulbs, and copies of itself in every zoom. [PAUSE]
You can move closer forever and still find detail. [PAUSE] It is as if a two-line
equation opened a door to a visual universe. [PAUSE] And the curious part: the
boundary between what escapes and what stays bounded is exactly where the beauty
concentrates.
